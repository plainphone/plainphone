# Family communication in Plainphone

Start with an optional **Ask a parent to call** action on SOS (3–5 agent-days using existing Web Push and carrier calling, including a caregiver-entered callback number for the phone). Then build a quiet, invite-only family inbox around the existing authenticated API. Start with short coordination messages from #109 and add endpoint-encrypted text before photos or voice notes. If families need calling without mobile service, try a tap-to-answer, child-initiated two-way LiveKit audio pilot on web-5 (8–13 agent-days with a browser answer; a native parent app receiver adds more, see the estimates). Ringing on locked parent phones is a separate reliability investment. Keep normal caregiver calls and 112 available throughout.

**LiveKit estimate breakdown.** 1–2 days for SDK/relay proof on physical Android and a parent browser; 2–3 days for the existing API to route an expiring invitation and mint two role-scoped, single-room credentials; 3–5 days for Android and browser UI, permissions, toggles across both caregiver clients and answer/cancel handling; 2–3 days for network, security and UX verification. Reserve **+3–5 days, unverified**, for interoperable endpoint-owned media keys and verification. This allowance is not standalone. It assumes authenticated device keys and trusted device admission already exist, but README.md and docs/encryption.md still describe those as a specification under development. The prerequisite is draft 3 (endpoint encryption design, 5–8 days) plus its enrolment implementation. If the pilot needs endpoint-only media before that ships, add that work. Keep two pilots apart: an **SFU-trusted pilot** (the 8–13 day estimate, where the server operator can in principle hear media, for adult synthetic use or disclosed family use) and a **same-privacy comparison** with endpoint-only media (needs the prerequisite plus this allowance). Default SFU transport encryption terminates at the SFU. An adult-only synthetic pilot can validate transport first. Gate family use on the documented trust model and privacy review. [LiveKit E2EE](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/encryption/)

**Why 23–38 was plausible.** PR #111 budgeted 4–7 days for cross-platform proof with cold-start ringing, 6–10 for voice protocol/signalling/push/relay, 8–13 for native media and ringing UI, and 5–8 for review and hardening. LiveKit takes over media negotiation, ICE and relay, and a foreground browser answer avoids native parent media and ringing in the first slice. Limit the pilot to one caller, one parent endpoint, audio only, one attempt and short expiry. Defer iOS and Android native receiving, inbound child calls, group calls, video, persistent call history and multi-parent ringing. Keep authorization, cancellation/expiry checks, microphone consent, accessibility and carrier conflict tests. The ringing allowances above cover only wake-up, call UI and accept/decline. They do not include answering inside the Expo app: `parent-app/package.json` has no LiveKit or WebRTC dependency, so a native receiver needs the LiveKit React Native SDK and Expo config plugin, a development build instead of Expo Go, and native audio-session handling (CallKit on iOS, Telecom on Android). Price that separately at **+3–5 days, unverified**, shared by both platforms with most of the risk on iOS, where an iPhone can pass the PushKit/CallKit ringing test while answering still fails without SDK integration. Native ringing and answering on both platforms then brings the LiveKit estimate to **18–30 days** with push ready, or **20–34** if shared push work is missing, before additional E2EE work. Acceptance needs a physical locked iPhone and a physical locked Android parent phone, each receiving a call and carrying two-way audio with the child phone after accept. Treat that as a separate product decision.

For either media path, issue an opaque invitation and recheck policy, recipient and expiry at accept. Accept and cancel atomically, reject replay and stale media credentials, and distinguish sent, opened, answered and two-way audio. Keep mic off until explicit action. Exercise locked/unlocked devices, background/force-stop behavior, carrier interruption, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi/mobile change, forced relay and revoked endpoints. Only claim the behaviors supported by measurements. Do not call live 112 in testing.

### Cash and capacity

Existing web-5 means **€0/month incremental host rent** under the assumption that bandwidth, ports, certificates and CPU headroom fit the existing contract. No usage-based provider is assumed. Plan a single-node pilot with bounded concurrency, short-lived credentials, restricted relay quotas and minimal logs. Verify UDP ingress and relay ranges, firewall, WSS and TURN/TLS, public certs, any collision with the existing HTTPS listener on 443, and monitoring before deployment. LiveKit needs an SFU listener and embedded TURN as configured. The thin path needs Hono signalling and a separate authenticated coturn instance. Keep TURN off the ordinary HTTP proxy path. [LiveKit ports](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/self-hosting/ports-firewall/) · [coturn](https://github.com/coturn/coturn)

Illustration with decimal GB and assumed **64 kb/s per direction** including packet overhead. A 1:1 relayed audio call is about 0.96 MB/min outbound and the same inbound. At 100 families × 60 minutes/month, an SFU forwards about **5.8 GB outbound and 5.8 GB inbound** before extra TURN hops/retries. If 20% of peer-to-peer minutes need one relay hop, server transfer is about **1.2 GB each way**. At 1 Mb/s per video direction, the same all-forwarded usage is **90 GB each way** before overhead/retransmission. Ten simultaneous audio calls are about 1.28 Mb/s in each direction at the SFU. These are planning assumptions, not codec guarantees or measured relay fractions. Meter NIC usage, packet rate, CPU and jitter in a capped synthetic trial. Check billing and headroom before expanding. Provision **0.5–1 agent-day/month** initially for monitoring, updates and incident triage, unverified. Extra bandwidth or a host change would need a new estimate.

## Family inbox

Start with one quiet thread per approved caregiver relationship. A child can send a short preset or text, see who receives it, and tell queued from delivered to a device. The parent can reply. Avoid read receipts, last-seen, typing indicators, streaks and public profiles. Use notification summaries or generic alerts outside quiet hours. High-priority SOS remains an explicitly separate category. “I'm home” is a user-sent check-in with no location attached by default. Later add a one-time, explicitly confirmed location snapshot with clear recipients and expiry. Keep #109's code discreet to bystanders and its meaning known to the child. A delayed ordinary message may sync later. A stale SOS must retain its original timestamp and use the SOS policy for any late alert.

Add static emoji reactions and a small built-in sticker set after text. Add deliberate voice recording with a visible timer, preview, discard and recipient confirmation, then bounded photos with previews and separate enablement. Expand to siblings and grandparents by named opt-in invitation with a child-visible roster and simple revoke/block. Shared rooms can follow only after the membership and key lifecycle are tested. New members start at the point they join. Approving a contact does not enroll that contact as a caregiver.

In the recommended design, the endpoint roster lists and verifies recipient devices. A new device needs visible approval and fresh keys. Revocation terminates future access and rotates conversation secrets. Recovery is a visible re-enrollment or an explicitly enabled user-held encrypted backup after review. Server queues store ciphertext, recipient IDs, timestamps and bounded delivery metadata. Neither service operators nor parents outside a conversation get decryption keys. Web dashboard management can ship independently of browser decryption. Offer a browser inbox only after the trust and key-storage model has been reviewed.

### Privacy and safety

A preregistered Dutch 100-day diary study with 479 adolescents found that parent messaging was common but varied widely, and covered support and practical coordination as well as monitoring. In a small study of 19 parent-teen pairs, family oversight tools enabled transparency and discussion but built-in messaging had limited appeal. A qualitative study of 13 parent-child pairs found concrete perspective-taking exercises supported discussion about privacy. These support a focused logistics use case and visible rules, with limited evidence that this product would replace commercial messengers or improve outcomes. [Diary study](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.14203) · [Joint oversight study](https://doi.org/10.1145/3512904) · [Privacy discussions study](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/wen25supporting.pdf)

- **Younger children:** start with caregiver conversations and visible recipient chips. Caregivers read conversations they participate in and help agree the roster. Use plain wording on the phone showing who receives messages and which settings parents can see or change.
- **Growing independence:** offer opt-in approved sibling and grandparent threads, keep content with participants, and schedule a family review. Teenagers should be able to decline invitations, block or ask for help, and see caregiver changes. Keep emergency and confidential helpline routes independent. Avoid fixed birthday-based permission changes and hidden copies, escrow or remote message browsing. [Plainphone design principles](../design-principles.md) · [UN General Comment 25](https://www.ohchr.org/en/documents/general-comments-and-recommendations/general-comment-no-25-2021-childrens-rights-relation)
- **Known contacts still pose risk.** Enforce approved, mutually accepted relationships on send and read, with rate limits, account recovery protection and instant visible revoke. No strangers, public groups, directory, anonymous invites, unrestricted links or forwarding. Offer a trusted-adult/help route and deliberate minimal reporting with a preview of what is shared. Have counsel define the process for suspected abuse. Avoid automatic forwarding to a caregiver who may be involved.
- **Photo protection.** Keep photos off until the safety design passes review. Strip EXIF/GPS on device, re-encode and downscale locally, limit dimensions and size, preview every send, and keep media blur by default on receive. Use fully on-device sensitivity detection for possible intimate images, before encryption on send and after decryption on receipt. For managed child phones, block flagged outgoing images while protection is enabled and offer a discreet path to help without sending the image or alerting the caregiver; withhold previews on receive. Allow a child-visible per-phone protection setting with protection on by default when managed photo sharing is enabled. If disabled, disclose the reduced protection when enabling photos. No remote classifier, provider scan, telemetry about classifications or automatic report. A classifier cannot determine age or reliably stop all abuse. Validate false positives, missed detection and bypasses with lawful synthetic test assets, across device speeds; keep photos off in the pilot if protection is unproven. Apple documents a local warning/blur pattern; Android implementation and efficacy are unverified. [Apple Communication Safety](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/105069)
- **Quotas and deletion.** Pilot proposals, subject to review: 2,000-character text; 2 MB re-encoded photos; 60-second/1 MB voice clips; 100 MB per family; 30-day rolling text, 14-day media, 24-hour callback events and minute-scale live invites. Count ciphertext and pending uploads. Delete expired data and orphaned uploads in a bounded job with restore-time expiry checks. Track local/queued/server deletion separately and disclose that recipients may keep copies. Offer delete on device, contact removal and account deletion. Set and test a finite encrypted backup expiration before rollout. Keep location snapshots short-lived and outside media metadata.

### Mode and toggle contract

Light mode uses the family inbox beside normal calling. App contact restrictions apply inside Plainphone; carrier/other app restrictions depend on available platform permissions and must be explained. Strong mode starts with presets, caregiver text and visible check-ins. Add media and calls independently after Device Owner, lock task, Private DNS, foreground audio and emergency reachability tests. Myself phones are paired Self phones that already have a dashboard settings path, with rules governed by the adult's own key (see `web/src/pages/PhoneSettings.tsx`). Keep adult messaging outside the first phases. If it is added later, it uses the same per-phone toggles in the adult's dashboard and parent app, synced through `/rules` under the adult's own key, with no caregiver role or monitoring by default. The adult can leave at any time. Caregiver controls for a Child phone are unchanged.

Every optional capability has a per-phone setting in both caregiver surfaces with a default and one-line explanation, synced through `/rules` and enforced on the child phone. Publish child-visible changes. Default new callback, presets, inbox, reactions/stickers, check-ins, snapshots, photos, voice notes, each app-call direction and video to off on existing phones. Photo protection defaults on when photos are enabled. Use separate per-contact consent as well as the phone gate. Quiet/preview and notification options are explicit. Device-side gates stop new actions after sync. Accept and record authenticated reports for actions already performed with original timestamps and notify appropriately even if rules changed in transit. This is distinct from authorizing a new recipient, upload, retrieval or call. Keep 112 and existing parent reachability available.

## Denmark/EU — needs a lawyer before a family pilot

- **NI-ICS classification unverified for Plainphone.** EECC Art. 2 covers normally remunerated interpersonal, interactive communication among finite recipients chosen by participants and includes a narrow ancillary-feature exception. A publicly offered, monetized family inbox or app voice/video with no public-number interconnect looks like NI-ICS despite private rosters. Assess remuneration, free/paid bundling and ancillary status. A callback hands voice to a carrier, with a separate assessment for the in-app request itself. Check Danish Telecommunications Act and the personal-data security and end-user orders, including confidentiality, incident rules and any specific microenterprise exceptions. [EECC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2018/1972/oj) · [Danish Act](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2025/681) · [Privacy order](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2020/1882) · [End-user order](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2023/566)
- **GDPR roles, basis and retention unverified for each deployment.** Mahope likely acts as controller for its hosted service where it sets purposes and means. A self-hosting organization may control its own processing. Software publication alone and genuinely household-only use have different implications. Determine roles for relays, push, telemetry and any hosted support by actual data flows. Check Art. 6 basis per purpose, child-facing notices, processor terms, transfers, DPIA, erasure, confidentiality and any separate traffic-data retention or lawful-disclosure obligations. Proposed TTLs above are product choices pending review. **Denmark raised its Art. 8 threshold from 13 to 15 on 1 January 2024** for consent-based information society services offered directly to children. Below 15, holder-of-parental-responsibility consent/authorization applies in that case. This is not a universal minimum age and counsel should check the basis and contractual capacity. [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) · [Danish § 6](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2024/289) · [Amendment](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/ft/A20230178330)
- **CSAM.** The prior 2021/1232 interim derogation expired in April 2026. The EU adopted a successor temporary derogation, Regulation (EU) 2026/1881, for certain voluntary processing by NI-ICS providers, effective 31 July 2026 through 3 April 2028. Parliament’s summary describes the exclusion of audio and end-to-end encrypted communications and the need for a separate GDPR legal basis. Its applicability to Plainphone and specific detection safeguards or reporting workflows need counsel confirmation. The long-term CSA proposal is a separate legislative file. Parliament’s tracker still lists first reading as pending. Final obligations remain unverified. Establish a lawful incident and reporting process before photos. Do not infer a blanket scan duty or permission from a proposal. [Council reasons](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AC_202603747) · [Temporary regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R1881) · [Parliament summary](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1910828) · [Long-term procedure](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2022/0155%28COD%29)
